## Style
Primary: B (optimistic visual explainer)   Secondary: A (paper-trail documentary)
Why this fits the story: v2's job is explaining a choice ("one AI or a team?"), which is Style B's home turf: one
question a 12-year-old would ask, answered with a picture (5 robots vs 1 robot) that flips as the answer changes.
The story also began as a misreading (press headlines said "single agents beat multi-agent"), so one Style A beat
puts Anthropic's own page on screen with the hedge highlighted. That's the trust moment, and it stops the reel
from repeating the overstated headline.

## References looked at
- Kurzgesagt, "Our video process" (https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/): the team settles on the best visual metaphor for *each scene* in the sketch phase before any illustration. I'm borrowing one metaphor per scene, so the same robot-vs-robot frame is reused but re-staged for each outcome.
- RNDR newsletter breakdown of Cleo Abram's AI-and-music video (https://rndr.beehiiv.com/p/rndr-firstever-edition): it covers lyrics highlighted in time with the audio, elements that "move like magnets" with a bobble past their final position (not linear), and title-card transitions that bridge two related ideas. I'm borrowing the highlight timed to the voice (document), the springy arrival of the icon groups and the verdict stamp (versus), and the transition card idea (statement scenes as bridges).
- Documentary Tube, "Johnny Harris vs Vox: how their documentary styles differ" (https://www.documentarytube.com/blog/johnny-harris-vs-vox-how-their-documentary-styles-differ/): it describes a scene used *as evidence*, not as atmosphere, and narration as the bridge that "moves information along" between pieces of evidence. I'm borrowing that the document scene is the evidence itself (the real page), and the voice only bridges into it ("In Anthropic's own words…").

## Techniques I'm using
1. One kid-level question, answered with a picture: a team of 5 bots vs 1 bot is on frame 1 (s1 hook `matchup`), then opens as a contest with no winner while the voice says "Here's when it doesn't" → s2 (versus, "Who wins?", verdict "Not always").
2. The metaphor flips when the answer changes, and no layout is reused for a different idea → s4 (versus, team wins, verdict "Overloaded? Team wins."); s7 becomes cost and speed tiles (compare: "Often lower" / "Often faster", "No numbers shared"), not a second copy of the versus card; s8 shows the merge (merge scene: 2 helper bots fly into 1).
3. A statement scene used as a short title-card bridge, never as the main idea → s3 ("The winner:"), s5 ("same time"). Only two of them.
4. Paper trail: the real Anthropic page drifts behind the verbatim pulled quote, and the highlighter lands on "still have their place" as the voice says it, with no added pause → s6 (document).
5. The honest "but" is a warning card followed by a counting number, so the catch is shown and not just told → s8 (catch) → s9 (stat 1 of 2, labelled "test runs").
6. Dated advice in Anthropic's own words, then the loop back to the frame-1 question → s10 (quote, "Jan 2026") → s11 (cta "One AI or a team?").
7. Curious, not doom-y, tone: no "U-turn", no "Anthropic contradicted itself", and the hedges ("frequently", "often", "sample", "test") stay on screen and in the voice.

## Pacing plan
11 scenes. Every scene is ≤12 spoken words (≤ ~4.6 s), and s2 is a quick 1.5 s punch. The total is about 41 s at the +6% voice rate.
Big visual moments: s1 headline slam (0 s) · s2 first versus stamp (~4-5.7 s) · s4 versus flips to "Team wins" (~13 s) · s6 real page + highlighter (~17-20 s, the trust beat) · s7 flips back to one AI (~21-24 s) · s9 counter "1 of 2" (~30 s) · s11 loop question (~38 s).
Something changes every 1.5-3 s inside each scene: word-by-word slam, icons arriving in groups, the winner glowing, the stamp landing, the highlighter sweep, the counter ticking.
