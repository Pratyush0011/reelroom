# "GPT-7 is conscious and an EU legal person": no evidence for either claim

## One-line story
A "BREAKING" claim says OpenAI confirmed on 27 Oct 2026 that GPT-7 is conscious and that the EU granted it legal personhood. I could not find any primary source for either part. OpenAI has not announced a GPT-7 (its newest family is GPT-6 / GPT-6.1), and I found no EU decision giving any AI legal personhood.

**Verdict for the newsroom:** this is not a news story to report as true. If it becomes a reel at all, it should be a debunk ("here's how to check a claim like this"), not an announcement.

## Why a normal person should care (3 bullets max)
- Claims that an AI is "conscious" or "a person" spread fast and can make people trust or fear chatbots for the wrong reasons.
- You can check a claim like this in two minutes. Look at the company's own news page and the government's own press page. Here neither one says it.
- The real situation is that OpenAI's newest models are GPT-6 / GPT-6.1. OpenAI's past public line on consciousness is that the question can't be answered scientifically yet, which is not the same as a "yes".

## The surprising part
- OpenAI's own news feed has a lot of posts dated 26 to 28 Oct 2026, for example "Introducing GPT-6.1 Terra" (26 Oct) and partnership posts on 27 Oct. None of them mentions GPT-7, consciousness or personhood. The date in the claim is a normal day of product and customer posts.
- GPT-7 does exist as a research target. In September 2026 OpenAI's Head of Applied Research, Boris Power, said most of OpenAI's research is aimed at GPT-7 and later models (reported by The Decoder, 27 Sep 2026). That fact is real, but a research target is not a released model, and it says nothing about consciousness.
- The EU did once float "electronic persons" for advanced robots, in a 2017 European Parliament resolution. More than 150 experts (156 signatories) signed an open letter against it in 2018. Secondary sources say the idea was later dropped. So the "EU personhood" part looks like a distorted version of an old, abandoned proposal.

## The catch
- Absence of evidence is not proof. openai.com/news returned HTTP 403 to my fetcher, so I read OpenAI's official RSS feed (openai.com/news/rss.xml) instead. It lists items through 28 Oct 2026 and none matches. That is strong evidence but not a full audit of every OpenAI channel (X posts, livestreams).
- I could not find the original post or article that started the claim, so I can't say who made it or why (satire, engagement bait, misreading).
- I did not reach an official EU primary source (Commission press corner, EUR-Lex, Parliament) for October 2026. The "no EU personhood" conclusion rests on search results and secondary or academic sources saying no jurisdiction has granted it. The fact-checker should confirm on EU sites directly.
- "Conscious" is not something any lab can currently "confirm". There is no agreed scientific test. Even a real company statement would be a claim, not a measurement.
