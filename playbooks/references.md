# Reference styles playbook (director)

Great short-form explainers don't look like slideshows. Before you storyboard, pick **one primary style and
at most one secondary style** from the four below, look at 2–3 current real examples of it, and write down
exactly which techniques you're borrowing. Borrow *techniques* (pacing, framing, how evidence is shown),
never a creator's identity, catchphrases, logos or footage.

## Style A: Paper-trail documentary
*In the spirit of: Johnny Harris, Vox explainers.*

The story is told through the evidence itself: the page, the document, the quote.
- **Show the receipt, don't describe it.** Put the real source page on screen and pull the key sentence out of it, highlighted as it's spoken (`document` scene).
- **Scrapbook texture:** paper cards slightly tilted, grain, things dropped onto the desk (`receipt`, `document`).
- **Zoom into the detail that matters:** one highlighted phrase, not a paragraph.
- **Narration sounds like a curious investigator:** "So I went and read the actual post."
- **Best for:** "what they actually said", misreported stories, policy and legal news.

## Style B: Optimistic visual explainer
*In the spirit of: Cleo Abram's Huge If True, Kurzgesagt-style clarity.*

One big question, answered with a picture you can see in your head.
- **Open on a question a 12-year-old would ask**, then answer it with a **visual metaphor** (`versus` with icons: a team of robots vs one robot with tools).
- **One concept per scene**, built step by step. Never show the whole system at once.
- **Concrete comparisons:** "about as long as a phone call", "like hiring five interns vs one expert".
- **Tone is curious, not doom-y:** "here's the surprising part."
- **Best for:** how something works, A vs B, "is this a big deal?"

## Style C: Fast creator reel (India tech-news energy)
*In the spirit of: Varun Mayya / Indian AI-news creators.*

Punchy, opinionated, built for the scroll.
- **Something changes on screen every 1.5–2 s:** short scenes, numbers slamming in, punch-ins on every cut.
- **Big bold text that matches the voice word for word**; captions carry the energy.
- **"What this means for you"** stated plainly and early: students, jobs, money, India.
- **Short spoken sentences, 4–9 words.** No setup longer than one line.
- **Best for:** launches, price drops, "this changes X" news, career and money angles.

## Style D: Calm tech-reviewer clarity
*In the spirit of: MKBHD-style tech explainers, Tom Scott news explainers.*

Authority through restraint.
- **One clean hero visual per point:** a single big number, a single product or name.
- **Plain, confident narration;** no hype words; the facts do the work.
- **Generous negative space,** slow, smooth motion, nothing decorative.
- **Best for:** benchmarks, specs, comparisons where trust matters more than energy.

## How to research the references (each run)

1. Pick the style(s) that fit **this story's job** (correcting a misreading → A; explaining a choice → B; launch/price news → C; numbers-heavy → D).
2. Run 2–3 web searches for current, popular examples of that style on a similar topic (e.g. "Johnny Harris explains", "Cleo Abram AI agents", "Varun Mayya AI reel", "explainer shorts AI agents"). Open what you can and note concrete techniques: shot length, how evidence is shown, how the hook is staged, how the ending lands.
3. Write `<run_dir>/05b-direction.md`:
```
## Style
Primary: A (paper-trail documentary)   Secondary: B (visual metaphor)
Why this fits the story: ...

## References looked at
- <title> (<url>): what I'm borrowing, in one line
- ...

## Techniques I'm using
1. ... → in scene s3 (document)
2. ...

## Pacing plan
Target scene length ≤ 5 s; total N scenes; where the big visual moments land (seconds).
```
4. Then storyboard with the scene library, applying those techniques.
